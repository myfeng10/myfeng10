# EchoMem Group Outreach Research

A real early-customer research pack for one specific product question:

> When useful context is trapped inside one teammate's AI conversation, how can it reach another teammate without making everyone's full conversation history company data?

[← Back to the worked outreach example](../../skill-examples/write-high-signal-outreach.md)

## The product moment

Before scheduling another catch-up meeting, Bob asks his own agent:

> What did Sarah decide about the billing migration?

The agent finds one context item Sarah intentionally published, shows Sarah as the owner, and links the original source. Her other AI conversations remain private.

**Share the useful context, not the whole conversation.**

## What this pack helps you do

It turns “find teams that might need memory” into a finite workflow:

```text
Find a public pain signal
→ score whether the workflow is real
→ write down why now
→ show one product moment
→ ask for one bounded discovery or pilot
```

The ideal early customer is not simply a founder, CTO, or small team. It is a team where multiple people use AI and have already paid a visible coordination cost: duplicated work, manual context handoffs, extra check-ins, fragmented decisions, or privacy concerns.

## Start here

1. Use the [ideal-customer rubric](01-ideal-customer-rubric.md) to score a public signal out of 18.
2. Before contacting anyone, write four lines: `Signal`, `Pain hypothesis`, `EchoMem connection`, and `Why now`.
3. Use the [outreach rubric](02-outreach-rubric.md). A message below 10/14 does not ship.
4. Follow the [playbook](03-outreach-playbook.md) from comment or DM through discovery and a 3–5 person pilot.
5. Borrow structure—not copy—from the [message library](04-message-library.md).
6. Track the exact hook, fact-versus-hypothesis boundary, score, and next action in the [lead tracker](06-lead-tracker.csv).

## One concrete qualification example

**Public signal**

> “Context management was the biggest investment.”

**What is fact**

The person publicly described context management as an active cost.

**What is still a hypothesis**

Selective, source-linked sharing may reduce check-ins or duplicated work. The post alone does not prove that.

**Why contact now**

The signal is recent, describes a live workflow, and gives a legitimate question to ask. Without that “why now,” the message does not send.

**EchoMem connection**

If the cost comes from moving decisions out of individual AI sessions, a private-by-default published memory may reduce that handoff cost. This remains a hypothesis until discovery.

**First message**

> Hi {{name}} — “context management was the biggest investment” was the line that caught me.
>
> I’m curious where that cost shows up now: do teammates or stakeholders need someone to manually reconstruct decisions from individual Claude or Codex sessions?
>
> We’re testing a private-by-default model where a person publishes one useful, source-linked decision and a teammate can retrieve it through their own agent; the rest of the conversation stays private. Would you be open to a 20-minute workflow discussion? If the problem is real, we would set up one 3–5 person pilot ourselves.

This is a worked structure, not a message to send blindly. The exact hook, workflow question, and ask must be re-verified for the real person.

## Use the pack in 20 minutes

1. Put one exact public signal into the lead tracker.
2. Score it with the ICP rubric.
3. Write `Signal`, `Pain hypothesis`, `EchoMem connection`, and `Why now`.
4. Draft one message with one hook and one ask.
5. Score the message out of 14; below 10 does not send.
6. Record the next action: skip, observe, comment, DM, discovery, or pilot.

## The message standard

The first message has only five jobs:

1. point to one real hook
2. move beyond paraphrasing it
3. ask one unresolved workflow question
4. show one private-by-default product moment
5. offer one bounded next step

It does not say “saw you're a founder,” explain the person's own post back to them, list abstract features, invent traction, or ask to “pick your brain.”

## The pilot offer

- 3–5 people
- one repo, project, account, or workflow
- 4–6 weeks
- setup handled by the EchoMem team
- private by default
- one agreed success measure

Possible measures include fewer context-transfer meetings, less duplicated research, faster stakeholder answers, actual retrieval use, and whether anyone felt uncomfortable about sharing.
