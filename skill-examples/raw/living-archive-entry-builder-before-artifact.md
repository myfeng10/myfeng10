# When the Context Became Visible

**Time period:** July 2026

**Thread:** Building tools that help people notice when an AI system’s working state is becoming unreliable

**Pull:** I kept losing the thread during long AI coding sessions. At first, I blamed model quality. What I could not see was how much stale context had accumulated—for the agent and for me.

## What happened

I built a tiny context-health overlay that made repeated reads and context saturation visible. When the session had become too burdened to trust, the overlay offered a clean-start handoff instead of asking us to keep pushing through the same confused state.

The useful shift was not a better model or a more elaborate workflow. It was giving both sides a way to see that the shared context was degrading.

## What changed

- I stopped treating every loss of coherence as a model-quality problem.
- Repeated reads and saturation became observable signals rather than a vague feeling that the session was going wrong.
- A clean start became a deliberate handoff, not a failure or an abandoned conversation.
- I began to think of state visibility as part of AI UX: systems should expose when the conditions behind their answers are becoming unreliable.

## What this connected to

The overlay came from paying attention to my own confusion. The moment I stopped hiding that confusion—or trying to work around it silently—it became product evidence. The discomfort was not separate from the design process; it was the signal that led to the useful thing.

**Future-self note:** Do not dismiss confusion too quickly or conceal it behind more effort. When a tool repeatedly makes you lose the thread, make the invisible state visible. The product may be hiding inside the part of the experience you are tempted to blame on yourself.
